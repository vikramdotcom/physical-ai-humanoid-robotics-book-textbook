## Introduction

Actuators are the muscles of robots—they convert energy into motion. Choosing the right actuator technology is one of the most important decisions in robot design, affecting everything from strength and speed to efficiency and safety. Different applications demand different solutions: a surgical robot needs precision, a warehouse robot needs endurance, and a humanoid needs power density.

In this chapter, we explore the actuator technologies that make robots move, from the electric motors that power most modern robots to the hydraulic systems that give humanoids like Atlas their impressive strength.

## Theory Content

### Section 1: Electric Motors

Electric motors are the most common actuators in robotics due to their efficiency, controllability, and availability.

**DC Motors**: Simple, continuous rotation motors controlled by voltage.
- Easy to control speed and direction
- Good for wheels and continuous rotation

**Brushless DC (BLDC)**: No brushes, higher efficiency, longer life.
- Used in drones, modern robots
- Requires electronic commutation

**Servo Motors**: DC motors with position feedback.
- Precise position control
- Common in robot arms and hobby robotics

**Stepper Motors**: Move in discrete steps.
- Open-loop position control
- Common in 3D printers, CNC machines

:::note Key Concept
The choice between motor types depends on the trade-off between torque, speed, precision, efficiency, and cost for your specific application.
:::

### Section 2: Hydraulic Actuators

Hydraulic systems use pressurized fluid to generate force.

**Advantages**:
- Very high power density
- Can produce enormous forces
- Self-lubricating

**Disadvantages**:
- Heavy (need pump, reservoir, fluid)
- Risk of leaks
- Complex maintenance

**Applications**: Heavy-duty robots like Atlas use hydraulics for dynamic movements requiring high power.

### Section 3: Pneumatic Actuators

Pneumatic systems use compressed air.

**Advantages**:
- Clean (no fluid leaks)
- Lightweight actuators
- Inherently compliant

**Disadvantages**:
- Compressible medium makes precise control difficult
- Need air supply/compressor
- Lower force than hydraulics

### Section 4: Series Elastic Actuators (SEAs)

SEAs add a spring between motor and load for force sensing and compliance.

**Benefits**:
- Natural shock absorption
- Force measurement through spring deflection
- Safer human-robot interaction

**Used in**: MIT Cheetah legs, many research humanoids

### Section 5: Emerging Technologies

**Artificial Muscles**: Soft actuators mimicking biological muscle.
- Pneumatic artificial muscles (McKibben)
- Electroactive polymers
- Shape memory alloys

## Simulation Lab: Motor Dynamics

**Platform**: Google Colab
**Estimated Time**: 30 minutes
**Difficulty**: Intermediate

### Procedure

```python
# Lab 2.3: Motor and Actuator Simulation
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

print("=== Motor Dynamics Simulation ===\n")

# Simulate DC motor response
def simulate_dc_motor(voltage, R=1.0, L=0.01, Kt=0.1, Kb=0.1, J=0.01, B=0.001, dt=0.001, duration=1.0):
    """
    Simulate DC motor dynamics
    R: Resistance, L: Inductance
    Kt: Torque constant, Kb: Back-EMF constant
    J: Inertia, B: Damping
    """
    steps = int(duration / dt)
    current = np.zeros(steps)
    omega = np.zeros(steps)  # Angular velocity
    time = np.linspace(0, duration, steps)

    for i in range(1, steps):
        # Current dynamics: L*di/dt = V - R*i - Kb*omega
        di_dt = (voltage - R*current[i-1] - Kb*omega[i-1]) / L
        current[i] = current[i-1] + di_dt * dt

        # Mechanical dynamics: J*d_omega/dt = Kt*i - B*omega
        domega_dt = (Kt*current[i-1] - B*omega[i-1]) / J
        omega[i] = omega[i-1] + domega_dt * dt

    return time, current, omega

# Simulate at different voltages
fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(12, 8))

voltages = [6, 12, 24]
for V in voltages:
    t, i, w = simulate_dc_motor(V)
    axes[0].plot(t, i, label=f'{V}V')
    axes[1].plot(t, w * 60/(2*np.pi), label=f'{V}V')  # Convert to RPM

axes[0].set_ylabel('Current (A)')
axes[0].set_title('DC Motor Response')
axes[0].legend()
axes[0].grid(True)

axes[1].set_xlabel('Time (s)')
axes[1].set_ylabel('Speed (RPM)')
axes[1].legend()
axes[1].grid(True)

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

## Quiz

### Question 1

Which actuator type provides the highest power density?

A) Stepper motors
B) Hydraulic actuators
C) Servo motors
D) Pneumatic actuators

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Hydraulic actuators provide the highest power density, enabling dynamic movements in robots like Atlas.
</details>

### Question 2

What advantage do Series Elastic Actuators provide?

A) Higher speed
B) Force sensing and compliance
C) Lower cost
D) Simpler control

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: SEAs include a spring that enables force measurement and provides natural compliance for safer human-robot interaction.
</details>

### Question 3

Why are brushless DC motors preferred over brushed DC motors?

A) Lower cost
B) Higher efficiency and longer life
C) Simpler control
D) More torque

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: BLDC motors have no brushes to wear out, resulting in higher efficiency and longer operational life.
</details>

### Question 4

What is a disadvantage of pneumatic actuators?

A) Too heavy
B) Difficult to achieve precise position control
C) Risk of fluid leaks
D) Low compliance

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Because air is compressible, pneumatic actuators are difficult to control precisely compared to hydraulic or electric systems.
</details>

### Question 5

Which motor type moves in discrete steps without position feedback?

A) Servo motor
B) BLDC motor
C) Stepper motor
D) DC motor

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C**: Stepper motors move in discrete angular steps, allowing open-loop position control without feedback sensors.
</details>
