## Introduction

The materials and structural design of a robot determine its strength, weight, durability, and cost. A robot that's too heavy wastes energy and moves slowly; one that's too weak fails under load. Engineers must balance these competing requirements while considering manufacturing constraints and budget.

## Theory Content

### Section 1: Common Robot Materials

**Aluminum Alloys**: Lightweight, strong, easy to machine. The workhorse of robot construction.

**Steel**: Strong and inexpensive but heavy. Used for high-load components.

**Carbon Fiber Composites**: Excellent strength-to-weight ratio but expensive.

**Plastics**: Lightweight, moldable, good for housings and non-structural parts.

**Titanium**: Strong, lightweight, corrosion-resistant but expensive to machine.

### Section 2: Strength-to-Weight Ratio

The specific strength (strength/density) determines how much load a material can carry relative to its weight.

| Material | Density (g/cm³) | Yield Strength (MPa) | Specific Strength |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Steel | 7.8 | 250-500 | 32-64 |
| Aluminum 6061 | 2.7 | 276 | 102 |
| Carbon Fiber | 1.6 | 600-3500 | 375-2188 |
| Titanium | 4.5 | 830 | 184 |

### Section 3: Structural Design Principles

- **Minimize weight** while maintaining strength
- **Use hollow structures** where possible (tubes vs solid rods)
- **Place material where stresses are highest**
- **Consider fatigue** for cyclic loading
- **Design for manufacturing**

## Simulation Lab: Material Comparison

**Platform**: Google Colab
**Estimated Time**: 25 minutes

### Procedure

```python
# Lab 2.4: Material Properties Comparison
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

materials = {
    'Steel': {'density': 7.8, 'strength': 400, 'cost': 1},
    'Aluminum': {'density': 2.7, 'strength': 276, 'cost': 3},
    'Carbon Fiber': {'density': 1.6, 'strength': 1500, 'cost': 50},
    'Titanium': {'density': 4.5, 'strength': 830, 'cost': 30},
}

# Calculate specific strength
for mat, props in materials.items():
    props['specific_strength'] = props['strength'] / props['density']
    print(f"{mat}: Specific Strength = {props['specific_strength']:.1f} MPa/(g/cm³)")

# Visualization
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 6))
names = list(materials.keys())
spec_strengths = [materials[m]['specific_strength'] for m in names]
ax.bar(names, spec_strengths, color=['gray', 'silver', 'black', 'blue'])
ax.set_ylabel('Specific Strength (MPa/(g/cm³))')
ax.set_title('Material Specific Strength Comparison')
plt.show()
```

## Quiz

### Question 1

Which material has the best strength-to-weight ratio?

A) Steel
B) Aluminum
C) Carbon fiber
D) Titanium

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C**: Carbon fiber composites have the highest specific strength (strength/weight ratio).
</details>

### Question 2

Why is aluminum popular in robotics?

A) It's the strongest material
B) Good balance of weight, strength, cost, and machinability
C) It's the cheapest material
D) It never corrodes

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Aluminum offers a good balance of properties for robotics applications.
</details>

### Question 3

What structural principle reduces weight while maintaining strength?

A) Using solid rods
B) Using hollow tubes
C) Using thicker material
D) Using denser material

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Hollow tubes provide similar bending strength to solid rods with much less weight.
</details>

### Question 4

What is "specific strength"?

A) The exact strength of a material
B) Strength divided by density
C) The maximum load a material can carry
D) The cost per strength unit

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Specific strength is strength divided by density, showing load capacity relative to weight.
</details>

### Question 5

Why might steel still be used in robots despite lower specific strength?

A) It's always lighter
B) Lower cost and easier joining
C) Better corrosion resistance
D) Higher specific strength

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B**: Steel is less expensive, easier to weld, and suitable for high-load components where weight is less critical.
</details>
