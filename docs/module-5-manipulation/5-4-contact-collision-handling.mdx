## Theory Content

### Section 1: Contact Modeling

Contacts involve normal forces, friction, and compliance. Models predict forces from geometry and motion.

### Section 2: Collision Avoidance

Motion planning and reactive control keep robots from hitting obstacles and themselves.

### Section 3: Safe Interaction

When contact is intended, compliance and force limiting ensure safety.

## Simulation Lab

```python
# Simple collision check
def collision_check(robot_pos, obstacle_pos, obstacle_radius):
    distance = ((robot_pos[0]-obstacle_pos[0])**2 + (robot_pos[1]-obstacle_pos[1])**2)**0.5
    return distance < obstacle_radius

robot = (1, 1)
obstacle = (1.5, 1)
radius = 0.3
print(f"Collision: {collision_check(robot, obstacle, radius)}")
```

### Question 1
Collision avoidance prevents?
A) Motion B) Unintended impacts C) Power use D) Communication
<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**: Collision avoidance keeps robots from hitting things unintentionally.</details>

### Question 2
Contact models predict?
A) Colors B) Forces from geometry and motion C) Sounds D) Temperature
<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**: Models predict contact forces for planning and control.</details>

### Question 3
Safe interaction uses?
A) Maximum force B) Compliance and force limiting C) No sensors D) Rigid control
<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**: Compliance and limits ensure safe contact interactions.</details>

### Question 4
Self-collision avoidance prevents?
A) External hits B) Robot hitting itself C) Power issues D) Network problems
<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B**: Robots must avoid collisions between their own links.</details>
